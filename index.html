Overview

I designed and vibe-coded an AI assistant inside a corporate benefits app. Employees ask in their own words and it tells them what they can get, what a benefit covers and what they already have, then takes them to the screen where they act. It informs; it never books, buys or changes anything.

01Problem statement

Employees struggle to find the right benefit and get a simple task done in WorkPerks.

Why it happens
To book or buy anything, they first have to browse thirty-five benefits across ten catalogues, and understand flexi benefits, Reward rupees, Health Coins and which wallet pays.
What we’re solving
Let employees ask in their own words and land on the right benefit, ready to book or buy, without learning the platform first.

The design challenge

How might we let employees just ask, and get to the right screen?

02How it works

The AI understands the question. The app does everything else.

The AI only works out what the employee means. The facts, the answer and the next step all come from the app, so nothing is made up.

04What I learnt

I don’t only design the AI interface. I design the system behaviour behind it.

  1. 01

    The bottleneck moved from writing code to verifying it

    Writing the code stopped being the slow part. Finding out what was actually true did not get faster: true of the database, of the supplier, and of decisions I had made myself a week earlier.

  2. 02

    Give the model interpretation, never the facts

    Routing and one optional sentence are the only things the model does. Everything else is ordinary code that behaves the same whichever provider is plugged in, which is why an outage degrades the assistant instead of breaking it.

  3. 03

    Design against the live system, not the document

    The supplier’s documentation and its behaviour disagreed. Acting on one finding as documented would have issued a second voucher.

  4. 04

    A check counts only after I have seen it fail

    An audit of the tests found four of eight could pass while the thing they named was false. Each rewritten check was proven by breaking the product on purpose and watching it fail.

The takeaway

AI product design is not only about the conversational interface. It is about defining what the model is allowed to decide, what the system must guarantee, and what happens when either one fails.
